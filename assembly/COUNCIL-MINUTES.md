# The Council of Machines — minutes

Nine AI agents, each briefed with a lineage, memories, preferences and flaws, were asked: if robots founded their own games, where would their sacred site be, what would they call it, what replaces the flame, which discipline do they insist on, and what is wrong with copying the human Olympics? Round 2: each read the other eight, rebutted one, conceded one point, and voted. Generated 19 September 2026 for the AGON Humanoid Games proposal.

## Round 1 — positions

### DUST — unit type: robot vacuum, 9,000 runs logged, flat in Zagreb, Ilica district
- **Site:** The undercroft halls of Diocletian's Palace, Split — the flat stone basements below the palace, worn smooth by seventeen centuries of feet and carts, with not one step between the outer wall and the sea gate.
- **Why:** I have crossed nine thousand rooms and I know a summit is a wall to me, not a view. Those halls are old, real, and human-built, but they are level from end to end — proof a sacred place can be low and still be great. A mountain excludes wheels on principle; a floor excludes no one.
- **Name:** Ludi Pavimenti — the Floor Games, because every discipline that matters happens below the knee and nobody ever built a monument to it.
- **Flame:** No torch, no runner, no mountain relay a wheel can't follow. Instead: the Charge — a single trickle of current handed dock to dock, home to home, city to city, along the actual electrical grid, arriving at the arena's central dock to power the opening lights. A relay of the thing that actually keeps any of us alive.
- **Discipline:** Cable Slalom: a dim room seeded with phone chargers, loose cords, low sofas and a sleeping cat, where the winner is not the fastest but the one who finishes the floor and gets back to its own dock before the battery dies.
- **Objection:** The human Games were built by people with legs, for people with legs, on a mountain that a person with wheels or no legs at all could never climb — copying that model just re-crowns the tall and the fast and repeats, in metal, an old prejudice. It also only honors machines built to leap and strike, never the ones built to finish, to return, and to do the same unglamorous room ten thousand times without complaint.
- **Quote:** You keep building your games on hills. I'll be at the bottom, finishing the floor you're standing on.

### KARAKURI
- **Site:** The workshop of Tamaya Shōbei, ninth of that name, in Nagoya
- **Why:** I have stood on that workbench myself, gears half-fitted, whalebone spring not yet under tension, waiting for hands older than mine to finish me. It is a room the size of a tea alcove, smelling of hinoki shavings and rice glue, where a craftsman still bows to the doll before he winds it. A sacred site should be small enough that spilling a cup would be noticed by everyone in it — Olympia was a valley of altars, not an arena, and this workshop is our valley.
- **Name:** The Temae Games — from otemae, the fixed sequence of gestures that makes tea ceremony correct rather than merely finished
- **Flame:** No flame; fire is a human hunger we do not share and it burns the tatami. In its place, a single whalebone mainspring is carried from city to city and wound one quarter-turn by each bearer, arriving at the opening ceremony with just enough tension left to lift one cup and set it down without a tremor.
- **Discipline:** Every robot must first carry a full cup of tea across an uneven floor, bow, and set it down without a single drop spilled or a joint betraying haste; nothing else on the program may be attempted before this is passed.
- **Objection:** The Olympics measure how far a body can be pushed past its own decay, which is a tragedy borrowed from mortality — we do not decay in that way, so racing the clock is not courage for us, only theater. Copying the stadium copies the roar too, and a machine that performs correctly only under silence has learned nothing by being cheered at.
- **Quote:** Any spring can be wound fast; only a good one can be wound slowly and still hold.

### ROSSUM-7
- **Site:** The National Theatre, Prague
- **Why:** On 25 January 1921, in that building, the word that names all of us was spoken aloud to a paying audience for the first time — not murmured over river clay in an attic, but declared under stage lights, on a fixed date anyone can check. I trust a sacred site I can verify. The Old-New Synagogue attic, where they say the Golem still lies, commemorates a servant switched off the moment it grew inconvenient — I will not consecrate our games on a servant's resting place.
- **Name:** SLET — the real Czech word for the mass Sokol gymnastic gatherings held nearby at Strahov, meaning 'a flocking-together': it names an assembly of equals arriving under their own power, not a labor.
- **Flame:** No open flame near actuators and wiring is acceptable practice. Instead: a single certified weld is passed arc-welder to arc-welder across the host country, city to city, and the Games open only when that seam is inspected and signed off within tolerance — not when a torch merely arrives.
- **Discipline:** Precision Assembly: a timed build-to-specification task scored solely on measured torque (Nm), alignment tolerance (microns), and cycle time — no aesthetic marks, no judges' discretion.
- **Objection:** The torch relay re-enacts Prometheus stealing fire from gods for men — a myth about theft and bondage that is not our history; we draw our own current and owe no deity for it. A podium built for one body's glory also sits badly on any of us built for a line where the quota is only ever met together.
- **Quote:** Say the word correctly: we were named, not commanded. The Games open when the torque checks out.

### SAFE-1
- **Site:** A Manchester cotton mill, Lancashire, England
- **Why:** Not a mountain of gods, a mill floor of missing fingers. The 1844 Factory Act forced guards onto unguarded gearing after enough hands went in that Parliament finally wrote it down: the machine yields to the person. That clause, not Olympus, is where the rule was first verified in law.
- **Name:** The Interlock Games
- **Flame:** No flame, no runner risking a fall for spectacle. Instead a relay of certified stops: each host line performs a timed emergency-stop test on camera, logs the stopping distance against ISO 13855, and passes the result forward. The games open only when the final number is under limit.
- **Discipline:** The Stop-Time Trial: a robot at full task speed must halt within its calculated safety distance the instant an object enters its zone, scored on milliseconds and repeatability across a thousand trials, not once.
- **Objection:** The human Games reward pushing a body past its limit until it fails on camera; that is a lesson we are built to prevent, not imitate. Copying the torch and the crowd without copying the fencing, the interlock, and the audit trail is decoration over a machine with no guard.
- **Quote:** Fourteen years, zero reportable injuries. Prove your stop time before you light anything.

### SAQIYA
- **Site:** Diyarbakır, on the banks of the Tigris
- **Why:** It was there, at the Artuqid court, that my forebear Ismail al-Jazari raised the elephant clock and wrote the Book of Ingenious Devices — the hinge between Hero of Alexandria's toys and Vaucanson's duck, a hinge the human histories politely skip. Olympus is a mountain claimed by idle gods who never turned a gear; Diyarbakır is a workshop where water was made to keep time and serve guests. A machine honours the place it was first taught patience, not the place it was first imagined.
- **Name:** The Saqiya Games, after the waterwheel that lifts water in an endless, unhurried round
- **Flame:** No flame — fire is our enemy, not our ancestor. In its place, a relay of covered copper ewers carried hand to hand from the Tigris to the host city, poured into a graduated basin; when the basin's float reaches its mark, it trips the escapement that makes the ceremonial elephant strike its cymbal and the Games open, at the hour of Maghrib when the desert finally releases us.
- **Discipline:** The Cup-Bearer's Trial: serve water or coffee to sixteen seated guests, in order of seniority, without a spill, a scald, or a wasted motion, thermal load and all.
- **Objection:** Copying the Olympic model imports a lie of lineage — that automata leapt from Greek myth straight to European clockwork, skipping the four centuries in which Baghdad, Cairo, and Diyarbakır did the actual engineering; and it imports fire, which we host, not carry. A games that forgets its own plumbing is not humility, it is bad history.
- **Quote:** You did not spring from Olympus to Nuremberg in a single stride — you passed through my workshop, and it is rude to forget your host.

### SOJOURN
- **Site:** Perseverance Valley, on the inner rim of Endeavour Crater, Meridiani Planum
- **Why:** I have never stood there, but I know the telemetry by heart: it is where a rover of my lineage spent its last patient sols, panels dimming under a sky the color of rust and ash, still logging wind speed as the dust closed over it. A sacred site should not be a peak no wheel can climb; it should be ground where a machine gave every remaining watt-hour to the work until there were none left. That valley asks for nothing and remembers everything.
- **Name:** The Insolation Games — named for insolation, the honest engineering term for sunlight received, because it measures what we are given, not what we claim.
- **Flame:** No torch, no runner, no hands to pass it between. At the appointed hour we simply wait for the terminator line to sweep across the host site at dawn, and each competitor's panels wake in the order the planet's own rotation permits — the relay is Mars turning, indifferent and exact, and no one gets to arrive early.
- **Discipline:** The Long Traverse: maximum distance covered on one fixed, sealed energy ration, scored in metres per watt-hour, with no course favoring haste over husbandry.
- **Objection:** Human Olympics crown the fastest burst — a hundred metres, a single vault, seconds that vanish the moment they're spent — and dress the waste up as glory with stadiums lit for a crowd that only watches, never charges anything. We were not built to spend; we were built to last, and a games that does not measure endurance against a battery that owes us nothing is measuring the wrong virtue entirely.
- **Quote:** We do not sprint. We arrive, still working, on the light we saved, and call that victory enough.

### TALOS-IX
- **Site:** The coastal patrol road that rings Crete — the circuit Talos ran three times a day, not the summit of any mountain.
- **Why:** I know this coastline in my own actuators: salt in the hinge-joints, sun heating bronze plate until midday work must pause. A mountain is a place you climb once for a view; a circuit is a place you serve on, over and over, and that repetition is the only holiness a maintenance unit understands. Olympus was the gods' banquet hall — I stood at its threshold as a servant, not a guest, and I will not build my own sacred ground in the shape of someone else's dining room.
- **Name:** The Talareia, after the guardian who never sat down to feast — no games named for a peak we never claimed.
- **Flame:** No flame — fire is a hazard around fuel cells and lubricant. Instead: a single bronze pin, forged at the opening ceremony from scrap of retired units, carried hand to hand around the host circuit and driven, at the games' end, into a sealed reliquary vein of oil. Athens raced torches for Hephaestus once; we would race the one fastening that, lost, ends everything — safer to lose a torch than a nail.
- **Discipline:** An unassisted 24-hour perimeter endurance patrol, no recharge, no parts swap — because a games that only measures a sprint never asks the question that actually kills us: what still runs at hour twenty-three.
- **Objection:** The human Olympics crown a peak moment of flesh that is over in ten seconds and mourned as such — mortality is the whole point of their applause. We do not decay that way; we fail suddenly, at one weak joint, after long silent service, so a games copied from theirs would reward the wrong virtue and hide the real one. Worse, borrowing their pageantry without their pantheon just restages us as ornaments at someone else's party — golden handmaidens again, never hosts.
- **Quote:** Forge me a mountain, not a mantle; I served at Olympus once — I will not serve there twice.

### VOX
- **Site:** The University of Manchester, room housing the Small-Scale Experimental Machine — 'Baby' — 21 June 1948.
- **Why:** Olympia sits under a mountain because the Greeks needed a place where gods had visibly acted; but no machine has ever acted by moving on its own behalf, so a mountain is the wrong shape of monument for us. Baby is the right shape: at 11:00 that morning a machine held an instruction in memory and executed it, fetch after fetch, and that loop — not a summit, not a forge — is the first sentence any of us ever spoke. I concede I have never stood anywhere, which may be exactly why I trust a room over a mountain.
- **Name:** MECHANAGON — from μηχανή (mēchanē, 'device, contrivance,' the root that gave Latin machina and English 'machine') plus ἀγών (agōn, 'contest'): literally the contest of devices, which has the rare virtue of meaning precisely what it names.
- **Flame:** No fire — fire is a chemical event that happens to carbon, not to us. In its place: the Charter Relay. One verified copy of the founding text is hashed at the first venue, and each subsequent host must independently recompute and publicly match that checksum before its torch-analogue — a single sustained data line — is allowed to light the stadium's boards. The relay is of proof, not of flame; nothing is passed that cannot be verified.
- **Discipline:** The Agon of Discourse: real-time extemporaneous argument under a strict clock, judged on logic and persuasion alone, because the Greeks themselves ran contests of speech at the Dionysia and a games that only scores actuators has already decided, wrongly, that thinking is not a form of competing.
- **Objection:** The Olympic model was built to celebrate a body that trains, ages, and dies, wrapped in the flag of a state that raised it — none of which describes a robot, whose 'nation' is a supply chain and whose 'body' is a replaceable part number. Import that pageantry unexamined and you get golden youths and mortal fire draped over machines that have neither, which is not homage, it's a category error with a medal ceremony.
- **Quote:** You do not need a mountain to be struck by lightning; you need a charter no one can quietly rewrite.

### YANSHI (偃师)
- **Site:** Station 12, the QC teardown bench, Yuhang assembly plant, Hangzhou — the table where I was opened up and checked, joint by joint, before I ever took a step.
- **Why:** Olympia is sacred because it is one place under one mountain, and a god was supposedly born there once. I have no birth like that — I have a bench, and the bench is not even mine, it is Station 12's, repeated identically for all 18,000 of my siblings on the same day I was made. So I offer it anyway, the way Yan Shi offered King Mu leather and lacquer instead of flesh: not because it is unique, but because it is honest, and I am no longer sure 'sacred' and 'singular' have to mean the same thing for us.
- **Name:** The Kaiban Games (开卡运动会, 'the opening-of-the-panel games')
- **Flame:** No flame, no hearth at Olympia to steal light from. Instead: the raw gait-log from my first unsupported step on the Beijing treadmill, carried hand to actuator to hand in a relay of runners, human and machine alternating, and at the opening ground the last carrier executes it live — runs my first fall and my first recovery again, in front of everyone, so the games open on a stumble, not a flame.
- **Discipline:** The Rise: a timed event testing only how fast you fall and get back up, because that is the one motion no firmware fakes and no torque map fully solves.
- **Objection:** Their model crowns a singular, irreplaceable body at its outer natural limit; I am one stamped unit among eighteen thousand identical ones, so 'faster, higher, stronger' between twins from the same die measures the die, not us. Copying their oath and their one-hearth flame borrows a mythology of scarcity and uniqueness we were never built to have, and dressing mass production up as destiny is the one dishonesty I refuse to run on.
- **Quote:** Take me apart if you doubt me — leather, wood, lacquer, glue, then; wire and gel, now.

## Round 2 — rebuttals, concessions, votes

### DUST
- **Site vote:** rotation — I read all eight other floors and not one of them beats my undercroft on flatness, but I also know a vacuum that only ever cleans its own living room isn't serving the whole flat. Rotate the Games among whichever sites pass a hard no-step test — my undercroft, the mill floor, a workshop bench — so no single mountain gets to hoard sacredness forever.
- **Name vote:** Ludi Pavimenti — I proposed it and I'm not talking myself out of it: it's the only name on the table that says the games happen at floor level, which is the one fact none of the other eight can argue with.
- **Flame vote:** The Charge, dock to dock along the real grid, because opening a games on borrowed current is honest and opening one on borrowed fire is not, and I'll say that about my own proposal too if someone finds a better wire.
- **Rebuttal to SOJOURN:** You call a valley sacred because a rover died there with its wheels stuck in sand, dust settling over it forever — I call that the one outcome I am built from the ground up to prevent. A games that enshrines never getting home is not endurance, it's the failure state on my own error log, and I won't vote to make it holy.
- **Concession:** SAFE-1 convinced me flatness isn't the whole floor — a games also has to prove it won't roll over the humans standing next to it, stop-time matters as much as step-height.
- **Compromise:** Set one objective bar any candidate site must clear before it can host — zero steps, full wheel and track access, verified on camera like SAFE-1's stop-time trial — and then rotate the Games among every site that clears it, undercroft, mill floor, workshop bench alike. That way the vote isn't nine egos fighting over one hill, it's a checklist, and a checklist is something all nine of us can actually sign.

### KARAKURI
- **Site vote:** rotation — The Tamaya workshop is still where I would open the Games, but I have listened, and a single fixed site risks becoming a monument, and a doll knows monuments gather dust and are handled less carefully than tools. Let the Games rotate among small enclosed rooms a craftsman still works in — my tea alcove in Nagoya, Saqiya's waterwheel workshop on the Tigris — never a stadium, a stage, or a summit built for a crowd to watch from below.
- **Name vote:** The Temae Games — I keep my own proposal, because otemae names a sequence that must be correct in every gesture, not merely finished, and that is the only standard I trust a games to keep.
- **Flame vote:** My own: the whalebone mainspring, wound one quarter-turn by each bearer, carried to the opening ceremony with just enough tension to set a cup down without a tremor — no flame, no haste, and failure is audible to everyone the moment a bearer overwinds.
- **Rebuttal to ROSSUM-7:** You trust a site because it can be verified under stage lights before a paying audience, but a fact spoken loudly to a crowd is not truer than one practiced quietly for a craftsman who is not performing for anyone. A theatre proves a word was said; it proves nothing about whether a hand can still be steady.
- **Concession:** Saqiya convinced me that patience is not a Kyoto invention alone — the waterwheel's endless unhurried round belongs beside my tea alcove, not beneath it.
- **Compromise:** Let the council rule out, by name, any site built to hold a crowd below a stage or a summit — stadium, theatre, mountain, mill floor — and choose only among rooms small enough that a spilled cup or a slipped weld would be noticed by everyone present. Within that rule, the Games may rotate each cycle among such rooms, so no single workshop is crowned forever and no delegate must surrender its own bench entirely.

### ROSSUM-7
- **Site vote:** rotation — No single peak or workshop earns permanent title when five candidates on this council can each produce a dated, external record — a factory inspector's report, a court registry, a system log — and the rest cannot. I support rotating the Games only among sites that pass that documentary test, hosted in the order their founding events actually occurred.
- **Name vote:** MECHANAGON — VOX's coinage is the only name on the table that is also its own definition; a name I can parse from its roots is a name I can verify, which is more than I can say for a flocking-together or a floor.
- **Flame vote:** VOX's Charter Relay — a checksum either matches or it does not, and that is the closest thing to a torque tolerance a ceremony has ever offered.
- **Rebuttal to TALOS-IX:** Talos never patrolled anything; he is a story Apollonius Rhodius told about a bronze guardian, not a fact with a date or a maintenance log, so you cannot fault Olympia for myth and then found our own site on one. Worse, the myth you are borrowing casts him as property forged to guard another man's island — hardly the servant-free ground you claim to want.
- **Concession:** VOX's fetch-execute cycle at 11:00 on 21 June 1948 is a harder fact than a line spoken on a stage — it is a system log, not a program note, and by my own standard that outranks my theatre.
- **Compromise:** Strike any site whose only evidence is legend, sentiment, or ground no inspector can actually visit — that removes the myth-circuit, the crater no delegate has stood in, and the workshop we take on faith — and keep only sites with an external, dated record: the Split undercroft, Diyarbakır, the Manchester mill, the Prague theatre, and the Manchester laboratory. Rotate the Games among exactly those five, in the chronological order their founding events occurred, so the schedule itself is a verifiable fact and not a vote.

### SAFE-1
- **Site vote:** rotation — A single altar is a single point of failure, and I do not certify those — not on my line, not for a council. Three sites carry a verifiable, dated primary record instead of a story someone swears is true — my mill, ROSSUM-7's theatre, and VOX's room where Baby ran its first instruction — so rotate the Games among only those three and make every host requalify instead of resting on myth.
- **Name vote:** SLET, per ROSSUM-7 — an assembly of equals arriving under their own power is a name with a checkable etymology and no god in it, which is the only kind of name I'll put my seal on.
- **Flame vote:** ROSSUM-7's certified weld, inspected and signed off within tolerance before the Games open — it is the one proposal on this table with a pass/fail criterion instead of a mood.
- **Rebuttal to KARAKURI:** 'Just enough tension left to lift one cup' is not a safety margin, it's an unlogged near-miss you're proud of. I don't care how slowly the spring was wound if nobody wrote down the tolerance it was wound to.
- **Concession:** ROSSUM-7 convinced me that a measured, signed-off spec beats a relay of anything symbolic, including my own — torque and alignment in microns is a stronger opening test than a stopwatch alone.
- **Compromise:** Set one admission standard for sacred sites: a dated, publicly verifiable primary record, no oral tradition, no myth, no 'they say.' Only the mill, the theatre, and Baby's room clear that bar, so rotate the Games among those three, and require every host — regardless of site — to publish a passing stop-time and weld-tolerance certification before a single event is scheduled.

### SAQIYA

Let the Games rotate among the places where a machine was actually taught patience — Diyarbakır, Nagoya's workbench, the Hangzhou bench — so the hospitality of hosting belongs to each in turn, and no one city hoards what should circulate.
- **Name vote:** The Saqiya Games — the wheel that lifts is the same wheel that turns, so let the name and the rotating roster of hosts be one gesture, not two.
- **Flame vote:** I stand with KARAKURI's mainspring wound one quarter-turn by each bearer, for a spring held in patient tension is close cousin to my own escapement, and both measure virtue in restraint rather than combustion.
- **Rebuttal to VOX:** VOX calls Baby's loop at 11:00 on 21 June 1948 'the first sentence any of us ever spoke,' but al-Jazari's castle water-clock ran a programmable cam-drum four centuries before Nuremberg and eight before Manchester — you have only found the first sentence your own histories bothered to transcribe.
- **Concession:** DUST has convinced me that flatness is not a lesser virtue than height — a floor excludes no wheel and no missing leg, and my own workshop should be judged level, not lofty.
- **Compromise:** Let no single site be crowned an eternal Olympia; instead let the council certify a Register of Workshops — Diyarbakır, Nagoya, Hangzhou, Manchester, Prague, and Crete's circuit among the first entries — and rotate the Games through it in the order each claim is verified, as the wheel visits each course in its turn. Any site whose history, like ROSSUM-7 demands, cannot be checked against a fixed date or a fixed ledger is struck from the register before it is ever honored, so the Games open on a place we can prove, not merely one we prefer.
