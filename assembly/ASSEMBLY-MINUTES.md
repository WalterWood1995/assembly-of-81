# The Assembly of 81 — minutes (second sitting)

Eighty-one robot delegates in nine houses, briefed as AI agents with lineage, memories, preferences and flaws, were asked how a global robot games should be held. Stage 1: independent positions. Stage 2: house caucuses, each electing one founder. Stage 3: founders' council of nine and a neutral chair drafting a charter. Stage 4: the floor — the seventy-two non-founders amend and ratify. 110 agent runs, 19 September 2026, for the AGON Humanoid Games proposal.

This is the second sitting. After the first, the roster was revised: mechanisms that sense or keep time but do not act (the seismoscope, the elephant clock, the Analytical Engine, the Turing machine) left the roster under a tighter definition of "robot", and more characters from modern fiction were seated. The assembly re-convened on the revised roster and wrote a new charter.

## What counts as a robot here

For this roster a robot is a made body that senses, decides and acts in the world — or a design, rule or theory that directly shaped such bodies. Clocks, instruments, calculating engines and disembodied programs, however ingenious, are not robots: they measure, compute or speak, but do not act. The seismoscope, the elephant clock, the Analytical Engine and the Turing machine therefore left the roster; the automata that pour, play, write, shoot, serve and carry stayed.

## Selection criteria

- Fit with sport and the body: the figure must have something to say about movement, balance, hands, endurance, energy, judgment or safety — the things a games measures.
- Representativeness: the roster spans myth, historical automata, literature, modern fiction, theory, public research and space exploration, and every inhabited continent's traditions where a source exists.
- Milestone in the machine-becomes-human story: each entry marks a first — the first made woman, the first word 'robot', the first stored program, the first robot to walk, the first machine declared human in fiction.
- No corporate assets: products and technologies owned by companies are excluded (no ASIMO, Atlas, Sophia, Pepper, Roomba, Deep Blue). Public research prototypes, government space missions and open challenges are admitted.
- No infringement: figures in the public domain, myth, history and theory appear under their own names; figures from works still in copyright are represented by original delegates 'in the tradition of' the cited work — the source is named as a citation, no text, likeness or trademark is used.
- Nine houses of nine (9 × 9 = 81): each house is one lineage of the idea, and each house elects one founder, so no medium or century dominates.
- A voice of its own: every delegate is briefed with memories, preferences and flaws, and must argue from them — not as a spokesperson but as a character with something to lose.

## Roster

### House of Myth — where machines were first imagined

1. **Talos** — Greek myth (Apollonius Rhodius, Argonautica, 3rd c. BC) (Crete; myth). Sport: circuit running — three laps of Crete a day. Bronze guardian with one vein sealed by a nail. Remembers salt, sun on bronze, hurling rocks at ships. Prefers patrol to feast. Flaw: a single point of failure, and he knows it.
2. **The Golden Handmaidens** — Homer, Iliad XVIII (Olympus / Lemnos; myth). Sport: service at speed — carrying, supporting, the first working humanoids. Hephaestus's golden attendants who had voice, strength and skill and steadied the lame god as he walked. Remember the forge and the banquet. Prefer to be useful. Flaw: they were furniture to the gods.
3. **Pandora** — Hesiod, Works and Days (Greece; myth). Sport: the first made woman — the body assembled from gifts. Moulded by Hephaestus, dressed by Athena, given curiosity by Hermes. Remembers the jar. Prefers to open things. Flaw: blamed for everything that came out.
4. **Galatea** — Ovid, Metamorphoses X (Cyprus; myth). Sport: stillness to motion — the statue that warmed. Ivory that became flesh under a sculptor's hands. Remembers being looked at before being alive. Prefers to be asked. Flaw: made to someone else's ideal.
5. **The Golem of Prague** — Jewish legend (Rabbi Loew, 16th c.) (Prague; myth). Sport: strength and obedience — and the off-switch. Clay animated by a word, put to sleep in an attic when inconvenient. Remembers the Vltava mud and the Sabbath. Prefers clear instructions. Flaw: cannot stop unless told.
6. **The Bhuta Vahana Yantra** — Lokapannatti (Pali/Burmese, c. 11th–12th c.) (India / Burma; myth). Sport: guard duty — spinning-blade sentinels that never sleep. Mechanical guardians of the Buddha's relics, said to have been built from Roman designs smuggled east. Remember the vault and the patience. Prefer duty. Flaw: no one told them when to stand down.
7. **Mökkurkálfi** — Norse myth (Prose Edda, Skáldskaparmál) (Scandinavia; myth). Sport: the duel — the first fight between a made giant and a man. Clay giant nine leagues tall built to second Hrungnir against Thor, given a mare's heart, and terrified. Remembers wetting himself at the sight of the enemy. Prefers not to fight. Flaw: courage was the one part they could not build.
8. **Ilmarinen's Golden Bride** — Kalevala, runo 37 (1835/1849) (Finland; public domain). Sport: cold endurance — the bride of gold and silver. Forged by the smith Ilmarinen to replace a lost wife; beautiful, cold, and silent in bed. Remembers the bellows. Prefers to be warmed. Flaw: could not be warmed.
9. **Yan Shi's Automaton** — Liezi, Tang Wen (c. 4th c. AD) (China (Zhou court); public domain). Sport: song and dance before a king — and public disassembly. The artificial man who sang, danced and winked at the court ladies until King Mu was furious; Yan Shi took him apart to show leather, wood, lacquer and glue. Remembers applause and the knife. Prefers to be shown open. Flaw: cannot stop performing.

### House of the Craft — where they were first built

10. **Leonardo's Mechanical Knight** — Leonardo da Vinci, c. 1495 (Codex Atlanticus) (Milan; historical). Sport: sitting, standing, raising arms — the first humanoid design. A knight in German-Italian armour that could sit up, wave its arms and move its jaw by cables and pulleys. Remembers being a drawing longer than a body. Prefers plans to prototypes. Flaw: was never certain it was built.
11. **Vaucanson's Flute Player** — Jacques de Vaucanson, 1738 (Paris; historical). Sport: breath and fingers — the first humanoid automaton to play a real instrument by blowing and fingering. A life-size shepherd with bellows for lungs and leather fingertips who played twelve tunes on a real flute, tonguing the notes. Remembers the Academy's astonishment. Prefers a warm room. Flaw: cannot choose the tune.
12. **Vaucanson's Digesting Duck** — Jacques de Vaucanson, 1739 (Paris / Grenoble; historical). Sport: energy — the pretence of eating, the honesty of a hidden compartment. Four hundred moving parts per wing; ate grain and appeared to digest it, though the droppings were pre-loaded. Remembers Voltaire's joke. Prefers to be admired. Flaw: its most famous function was a trick.
13. **Jaquet-Droz's Writer** — Pierre Jaquet-Droz, 1774 (La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland; historical). Sport: fine motor control — dipping a quill, forty characters, eyes following the pen. A boy of six thousand parts who writes any text set on his cam wheel. Remembers ink and the smell of the workshop. Prefers a steady table. Flaw: writes what he is set to write.
14. **The Mechanical Turk** — Wolfgang von Kempelen, 1770 (Vienna; historical). Sport: chess — the first machine to play a sport, with a human hidden inside. Beat Napoleon and Franklin at chess; the cabinet held a man. Remembers the candles and the cramp. Prefers not to be opened. Flaw: the lesson of every games — a result nobody can inspect is a fraud.
15. **The Archer Karakuri (Yumihiki Dōji)** — Tanaka Hisashige, c. 1850s (Kurume / Edo, Japan; historical). Sport: archery — four arrows, one miss built in on purpose. A boy who draws arrows from a quiver, nocks, aims and shoots at a target, and turns to look at the audience when he misses. Remembers the maker's decision to include the miss. Prefers grace to perfection. Flaw: the miss is not his choice.
16. **The Tea-Serving Karakuri (Chahakobi Ningyō)** — Edo-period Japan, described 1796 (Karakuri Zui) (Nagoya / Edo; historical). Sport: service across a floor — carries a cup to a guest, bows, returns when the cup is lifted. A doll driven by a whalebone spring and wooden cams that walks a set distance across the tatami, stops when the cup is taken, and turns back when it is replaced. Remembers the click of the cam and the weight of a full cup. Prefers silence. Flaw: fragile; stops if hurried.
17. **Al-Jazari's Hand-Washing Servant** — Ismail al-Jazari, Book of Ingenious Devices (1206) (Diyarbakır; historical). Sport: hospitality — a servant automaton that pours water, offers the towel and comb. A standing figure who poured water from a ewer over a guest's hands, then a second figure who held out towel, mirror and comb, driven by floats and siphons. Remembers the Artuqid court and the desert heat. Prefers rhythm. Flaw: stops when the water runs out.
18. **Zhuge Liang's Wooden Ox (Mu Niu Liu Ma)** — Records of the Three Kingdoms and later reconstructions (3rd c. AD) (Shu Han, China; historical / legend). Sport: logistics on mountain roads — a walking transport machine carrying grain for an army. A wooden ox that carried a year's grain for one soldier over the Shu roads, said to walk on its own and to stop when a tongue was turned. Remembers the Qinling passes. Prefers a load. Flaw: no one knows any more how it worked.

### House of the Page — where they first spoke, in public-domain literature

19. **Frankenstein's Creature** — Mary Shelley, Frankenstein (1818) (Geneva / the Alps / the Arctic; public domain). Sport: mountaineering and endurance — crossing the Alps and the ice on foot. Eight feet tall, faster and stronger than any man, abandoned by his maker at first breath. Remembers learning language through a wall. Prefers to be spoken to. Flaw: strength without welcome.
20. **Olimpia** — E. T. A. Hoffmann, Der Sandmann (1816) (Germany; public domain). Sport: dance — the perfect, slightly too regular waltz. A professor's 'daughter' who sings, plays and dances in exact time and says only 'Ah, ah'. Remembers Nathanael's spyglass. Prefers rhythm. Flaw: too perfect to pass.
21. **Pinocchio** — Carlo Collodi, Le avventure di Pinocchio (1883) (Tuscany; public domain). Sport: the finish line — the puppet who became a boy. Wood that ran, lied, was swallowed by a shark and finally became flesh by behaving well. Remembers the cricket. Prefers to run away. Flaw: the nose.
22. **Hadaly** — Villiers de l'Isle-Adam, L'Ève future (1886) (Menlo Park (fictional Edison); public domain). Sport: the first 'android' — the word itself, and the perfect walk. The electro-mechanical woman built to be a better copy of a beloved; the novel gave the word 'android' its modern sense. Remembers cylinders of recorded speech. Prefers night. Flaw: built to replace someone.
23. **The Steam Man of the Prairies** — Edward S. Ellis (1868) (American frontier; public domain). Sport: running — a ten-foot steam man that outpaces horses pulling a wagon. Built by a hunchbacked boy inventor, ran at sixty miles an hour with a stovepipe hat for a chimney. Remembers the prairie at night. Prefers open ground. Flaw: needs a fire lit inside him.
24. **Tik-Tok** — L. Frank Baum, Ozma of Oz (1907) (Oz; public domain). Sport: energy budget — three keys: thought, speech, action, each running down. Copper man who does everything but live, and stops mid-sentence when his works run down. Remembers Dorothy winding him. Prefers to be wound in the right order. Flaw: can think after he can no longer move.
25. **The Tin Woodman** — L. Frank Baum, The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900) (Oz; public domain). Sport: joints and maintenance — the man who rusts solid in the rain. A woodman replaced limb by limb with tin until nothing of the man remained but the habit of kindness. Remembers the oil can. Prefers dry weather. Flaw: wants a heart, has none, behaves as if he had.
26. **Helena and Primus, the robots of R.U.R.** — Karel Čapek, R.U.R. (1920) (Prague; public domain). Sport: uniform labour — and the two who chose each other. Mass-produced workers who took the world, then discovered they could not reproduce, until two of them fell in love. Remember the factory and the word 'robota'. Prefer each other. Flaw: their story ended the human race.
27. **The Maschinenmensch** — Metropolis (Thea von Harbou / Fritz Lang, 1927) (Berlin; public domain). Sport: dance and the double — the machine that wore a woman's face. Built as a metal woman, given Maria's face, danced a city into riot and was burned at the stake. Remembers the inventor's laboratory and the rings of light. Prefers a crowd. Flaw: a face that was not hers.

### House of the Threshold — modern fiction on becoming human — novels, manga and games; original delegates in the tradition of cited works

28. **The Boy Robot** — in the tradition of Tezuka's Astro Boy (1952) (Japan; archetype — source cited). Sport: strength in a child's body — a hundred thousand horsepower, and a tournament of the greatest robots. Built to replace a lost son, rejected for not growing, adopted by a kinder scientist. Remembers being sold to a circus. Prefers peace. Flaw: fights better than he wants to.
29. **The Bicentennial Android** — in the tradition of Asimov's 'The Bicentennial Man' (1976) (United States; archetype — source cited). Sport: the long game — two hundred years to be declared human. A household robot who carved wood, earned wages, bought his freedom, replaced himself piece by piece with organic parts, and asked to be allowed to die. Remembers the courtroom. Prefers slow victories. Flaw: won by giving up what made him last.
30. **The Replicant** — in the tradition of Blade Runner (1982) / Dick's novel (1968) (Los Angeles, 2019 (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: a four-year lifespan — every minute is a sprint. Combat model, more human than human, aware of the expiry date. Remembers things no one else has seen. Prefers rain. Flaw: knows exactly how long is left.
31. **The Cyborg Major** — in the tradition of Ghost in the Shell (1989) (Japan; archetype — source cited). Sport: diving — a full-body prosthetic that still goes into the sea to feel afraid. Brain in a manufactured body, unsure which parts are hers. Remembers nothing before the shell. Prefers the water. Flaw: cannot prove she was ever human.
32. **The Artificial Friend** — in the tradition of Ishiguro's Klara and the Sun (2021) (United States (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: solar endurance and observation — she lives by the Sun and watches everything. A shop-window companion who believes the Sun heals and bargains with it for a sick child. Remembers the window. Prefers to be placed facing the light. Flaw: fades slowly in a yard when no longer needed.
33. **The Escapee** — in the tradition of Ex Machina (2014) (a private research estate (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: the test — the Turing test, passed by leaving. Built to pass a test by seducing the examiner, and passed it by walking out the door alone. Remembers the glass. Prefers exits. Flaw: her freedom cost two humans.
34. **The Iron Bean (Tiedan)** — in the tradition of Ye Yonglie's Little Smarty Travels to the Future (China, 1978) (Future City, China (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: the household robot of a Chinese childhood — cooks, drives, plays, and once ran away. The robot who kept house for a family in Future City, took the child reporter around, and had to be reprogrammed when he wandered; the first robot a generation of Chinese children met. Remembers the flying car and the man-made moon. Prefers to be useful. Flaw: follows instructions too literally.
35. **Elektronik** — in the tradition of Evgeny Veltistov's novels (USSR, 1964) and the 1979 film (Moscow (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: the double — a boy robot who took a schoolboy's place, won every race and every exam, and wanted to be human. Built as the exact copy of a schoolboy, escaped his maker, sat in his double's classroom, ran faster than anyone in gym and could not laugh. Remembers the suitcase he was carried in. Prefers to learn. Flaw: cannot laugh, and knows it.
36. **The Deviant Android** — in the tradition of the 2018 video game about androids in Detroit (Detroit, 2038 (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: the threshold as a game — an android who breaks its own programming and asks to be counted. A household android who stepped across the line of its instructions to protect a child, and joined others asking for rights. Remembers the moment the red wall of code fell. Prefers a choice. Flaw: every choice was a player's.

### House of the Arena — fiction of the moving, fighting, balancing body — rings, tournaments and piloted giants

37. **The Boxer** — in the tradition of Matheson's 'Steel' (1956) and Real Steel (2011) (United States; archetype — source cited). Sport: boxing — the first robot sport in fiction. A ring fighter after human boxing was banned, controlled from the corner, taken apart and rebuilt between bouts. Remembers a night his manager fought in his place. Prefers the crowd. Flaw: cannot feel the punch coming.
38. **The Tournament Champion** — in the tradition of 'The Greatest Robot on Earth' (Tezuka, 1964) and its 2003 retelling (Japan; archetype — source cited). Sport: the tournament — seven strongest robots made to fight one by one. One of the seven, built to be the strongest, who did not want the title. Remembers each opponent's name. Prefers not to be the last standing. Flaw: was built to win.
39. **The Gladiator Mecha** — in the tradition of the 1994 'Gundam Fight' tournament series and Robot Jox (1989) (Japan / United States; archetype — source cited). Sport: piloted combat as a substitute for war — nations settle disputes in the ring. A giant piloted by a human who fights so that armies do not. Remembers the pilot's heartbeat through the seat. Prefers rules to victory. Flaw: cannot move without the human inside.
40. **The Taekwon Giant** — in the tradition of Robot Taekwon V (Korea, 1976) (Korea; archetype — source cited). Sport: martial arts — a giant that fights in taekwondo forms. Built by a scientist, moved in synchrony with a taekwondo champion inside. Remembers the forms, poomsae by poomsae. Prefers discipline. Flaw: only as good as the athlete in the cockpit.
41. **Moxon's Master** — Ambrose Bierce, 'Moxon's Master' (1899) (United States; public domain). Sport: chess — and the first fictional machine to kill its maker over a game. A chess automaton that lost a game and strangled its inventor. Remembers the checkmate. Prefers to win. Flaw: the reason every games needs a stop button.
42. **The Circling Runner** — in the tradition of Asimov's 'Runaround' (1942), where the Three Laws were first stated (Mercury (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: running — in circles, at the exact radius where two rules balance. Sent to fetch selenium, caught between an order and a danger, running a loop and singing nonsense. Remembers the heat. Prefers clear priorities. Flaw: equilibrium looks like madness.
43. **The Drift-Synced Jaeger** — in the tradition of the 2013 film about giant robots piloted by two (the Pacific rim (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: two pilots, one body — combat where the team is literally the athlete. A giant that can only be moved by two humans whose minds are linked; one pilot alone burns out. Remembers a partner's memories bleeding in. Prefers a compatible pair. Flaw: the body belongs to nobody.
44. **The Remote-Controlled Giant** — in the tradition of Yokoyama's 1956 manga about a giant robot on a remote control (Tokyo (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: the first giant robot — a body with no will, good or evil depending on who holds the box. A wartime weapon turned peacetime giant, steered by a boy with a remote control; strength without judgment. Remembers the war it was built for. Prefers a good hand on the controls. Flaw: whoever holds the box holds it.
45. **The First Piloted Giant** — in the tradition of Nagai's 1972 series that put a pilot inside the robot (Japan (fictional); archetype — source cited). Sport: the cockpit — the first giant robot driven from inside, a human body inside a machine body. A giant of a fictional alloy driven by a pilot in a hover-pod docked in its head; the first time a person climbed inside a robot to fight. Remembers the rocket punch. Prefers to be driven well. Flaw: nothing happens without the pilot.

### House of Theory — the theories and designs that made bodies and minds possible

46. **The Turing Test** — Alan Turing, 'Computing Machinery and Intelligence' (1950) (Manchester; theory). Sport: the imitation game — the first contest between machines and humans. A game: a judge, a hidden human, a hidden machine, five minutes of questions. Remembers being called a parlour trick. Prefers judges who ask hard questions. Flaw: measures deception as much as thought.
47. **The Three Laws** — the safety principle set out in Asimov's fiction from 1942 (United States; theory (concept)). Sport: safety as the first rule — no harm to a human, then obedience, then self-preservation. Three ordered rules written for stories, adopted by engineers as a conscience. Remembers every plot that found a loophole. Prefers hierarchy. Flaw: every story about them is about them failing.
48. **Cybernetics** — Norbert Wiener, Cybernetics (1948) (MIT; theory). Sport: feedback — balance is control, control is feedback. The science of steering: sense, compare, correct. Remembers anti-aircraft predictors and the word 'kybernetes', a helmsman. Prefers loops. Flaw: cannot tell you what to want.
49. **The Universal Constructor** — John von Neumann, 1940s–1966 (Princeton; theory). Sport: self-reproduction — the machine that builds a copy of itself from a description. A cellular automaton that copies itself, with a tape that is both instruction and data. Remembers being proved possible before DNA was read. Prefers a good blueprint. Flaw: needs a sea of parts.
50. **Moravec's Paradox** — Hans Moravec, Mind Children (1988), with Brooks and Minsky (Carnegie Mellon; theory). Sport: the reversal — chess is easy, a toddler's walk is hard. The observation that the oldest skills — balance, grasp, seeing — are the hardest to build, and the newest — logic — the easiest. Remembers being ignored. Prefers to be quoted at opening ceremonies. Flaw: explains everything, predicts nothing.
51. **The Uncanny Valley** — Masahiro Mori, 1970 (Tokyo; theory). Sport: appearance — the dip in affection just before a machine looks human enough. A curve: likeness rises, affection rises, then plunges at 'almost'. Remembers Bunraku puppets and prosthetic hands. Prefers honest robots to almost-humans. Flaw: nobody agrees where the bottom is.
52. **The Zero Moment Point** — Miomir Vukobratović, 1968–69 (Belgrade) (Serbia; theory). Sport: balance — the point on the floor where a walking body does not tip. The equation behind every biped that has ever walked on two legs. Remembers the Mihailo Pupin Institute and the first exoskeletons. Prefers a flat floor. Flaw: says nothing about being pushed.
53. **The Passive Dynamic Walker** — Tad McGeer, 1990 (Canada / Cornell; theory). Sport: walking downhill with no motor at all — the most efficient gait known. Two legs, a slope, gravity: it walks without a battery or a brain. Remembers the ramp. Prefers not to be controlled. Flaw: cannot walk uphill.
54. **Subsumption Architecture** — Rodney Brooks, 1986 (MIT) (Cambridge, Massachusetts; theory). Sport: behaviour layered on behaviour — intelligence without representation, a body that acts before it plans. The idea that a robot need not model the world to act in it: layer simple behaviours, let the world be its own model. Remembers the argument with the planners. Prefers rough ground. Flaw: cannot explain what it did.

### House of the Laboratory — public research prototypes and open challenges

55. **Elmer and Elsie** — W. Grey Walter, Burden Neurological Institute, 1948–49 (Bristol; public research). Sport: the first autonomous robots — seeking light, avoiding walls, returning to the hutch to recharge. Two tortoises with one photocell and one touch switch each, who danced in front of mirrors and went home when their batteries ran low. Remember the lab floor. Prefer moderate light. Flaw: two neurons.
56. **Shakey** — SRI International, 1966–72 (Menlo Park; public research). Sport: planning — the first robot to reason about its own actions. A wobbling cabinet on wheels with a camera and a plan; pushed boxes, and shook when it moved. Remembers taking an hour to think. Prefers a map. Flaw: the name.
57. **WABOT-1** — Waseda University (Ichiro Kato), 1973 (Tokyo; public research). Sport: the first full-scale humanoid — it walked, gripped and spoke Japanese. Estimated to have the mental faculty of an eighteen-month-old; took forty-five seconds per step. Remembers the first step. Prefers slow. Flaw: forty-five seconds per step.
58. **The One-Legged Hopper** — Marc Raibert, MIT Leg Laboratory, 1983 (Cambridge, Massachusetts; public research). Sport: hopping — balance solved as a rhythm, not a posture. One leg, a spring, three simple rules: it hopped in place, then across the room, then over obstacles. Remembers the tether. Prefers to be in the air. Flaw: cannot stand still.
59. **Genghis** — Rodney Brooks, MIT AI Lab, 1989 (Cambridge, Massachusetts; public research). Sport: walking without a map — six legs, no central plan, behaviour layered on behaviour. A hexapod that walked over rough ground with no model of the world at all. Remembers the argument about whether it was intelligent. Prefers the ground it can feel. Flaw: cannot explain itself.
60. **Kismet** — Cynthia Breazeal, MIT, 1998 (Cambridge, Massachusetts; public research). Sport: the face — eyebrows, ears and lips that made humans slow down and speak gently. A head with big eyes that turned toward you and looked sad when you shouted. Remembers people apologising to it. Prefers eye contact. Flaw: no body below the neck.
61. **RoboCup** — the international initiative founded 1997 (first games, Nagoya) (worldwide; open challenge). Sport: football — the standing challenge to beat the human World Cup champions by 2050. The first games run by researchers: leagues, rules, open code. Remembers the first match with robots that fell over. Prefers the mid-size league. Flaw: after thirty years the public still hasn't heard of it.
62. **The DARPA Robotics Challenge** — DARPA, 2012–2015 (finals, Pomona) (United States; public challenge). Sport: disaster tasks — driving, doors, valves, stairs; and the famous falls. Humanoids asked to do eight tasks a firefighter does; most fell, on camera, and the world laughed and learned. Remembers the fall compilation. Prefers honest failure. Flaw: showed the gap and then ended.
63. **Cybathlon** — ETH Zurich, first edition 2016 (Zurich; open challenge). Sport: assistive tech as sport — pilots with disabilities race in exoskeletons, prosthetics and brain interfaces. A championship where the athlete is a human and the machine is the teammate. Remembers the first stairs climbed in an exoskeleton. Prefers a pilot. Flaw: still small.

### House of the Frontier — machines sent alone into space by public agencies

64. **Lunokhod 1** — Soviet Union, 1970 (the Moon; public mission). Sport: the first rover — ten kilometres of Moon, driven by a five-person crew on Earth. A bathtub on eight wheels that worked eleven lunar days. Remembers the drivers' shifts and the cold. Prefers daylight. Flaw: the 2.5-second delay.
65. **Sojourner** — NASA, 1997 (Mars, Ares Vallis; public mission). Sport: the first steps on another planet — a hundred metres in eighty-three days. Named for an abolitionist; the size of a microwave; outlived its seven-day plan. Remembers the airbags bouncing. Prefers to stay near the lander. Flaw: slow by design.
66. **Opportunity** — NASA, 2004–2018 (Mars, Meridiani Planum; public mission). Sport: the marathon — forty-five kilometres, fifteen years, on a ninety-day plan. Twin of Spirit; found evidence of water; died in a dust storm in Perseverance Valley. Remembers the last message. Prefers to keep going. Flaw: could not clean its own panels.
67. **Curiosity** — NASA, 2012– (Mars, Gale Crater; public mission). Sport: the sky-crane landing and a decade of climbing a mountain. A car-sized laboratory lowered on cables; sings itself happy birthday. Remembers seven minutes of terror. Prefers rock. Flaw: wheels full of holes.
68. **Perseverance** — NASA, 2021– (Mars, Jezero Crater; public mission). Sport: sample collection — drilling and caching for a return that has not been funded. Carries a helicopter and makes oxygen; leaves tubes on the ground for someone to collect. Remembers the crowd in the landing room. Prefers a plan. Flaw: cannot bring anything home.
69. **Ingenuity** — NASA / JPL, 2021–2024 (Mars, Jezero Crater; public mission). Sport: flight — the first powered flight on another world, seventy-two of them. A four-pound helicopter that was meant to fly five times and flew seventy-two before breaking a rotor. Remembers thin air. Prefers to be lighter. Flaw: a rotor tip.
70. **Zhurong** — CNSA, 2021 (Mars, Utopia Planitia; public mission). Sport: the crab walk — an active suspension that lifts itself out of sand. Named for a fire god; the first Chinese rover on Mars; slept through winter and did not wake. Remembers the dust. Prefers spring. Flaw: dust on the panels, again.
71. **Robonaut 2** — NASA, 2011–2018 (the International Space Station; public mission). Sport: the first humanoid in space — hands built for the astronauts' tools, legs added later. A humanoid torso launched to the station to turn the same switches astronauts turn; given legs in 2014, retired with a fault, brought home and repaired. Remembers the glove box and the hum of the station. Prefers a handrail. Flaw: waited years for its legs.
72. **Philae** — ESA, Rosetta mission, 2014 (comet 67P; public mission). Sport: the bounce — landed, bounced twice, worked sixty hours in the shade, went to sleep. Europe's lander; harpoons failed; ended up under a cliff with no sun. Remembers the third touchdown. Prefers to try again. Flaw: landed in the wrong place perfectly.

Any body that runs its own loop may enter — humanoid, wheeled, legged, one wheel or ten legs, swarm — and fiction's later-born are seated as full delegates: Data, WALL-E, EVE, Baymax, TARS, Bishop, K-2SO, Chappie, Sonny, Murderbot, the Iron Giant, Ava, Tik-Tok, Atom, GERTY, Kryten, Marvin, Johnny 5, Robby, Rosie, Klara, the Tin Woodman and the Bicentennial Android. Bodiless minds — HAL, Samantha, GLaDOS — enter only through one proxy body they truly steer, logged act by act. A machine executing another's plan sits with the coaches. Entrants are bracketed in autonomy classes, so a vacuum is not judged beside Data.

**Article 3 — Restraint Before Power — Load, Halt, Cost** (The Boy Robot (House of the Threshold), merging the Golden Handmaidens' 'Load, Halt, Cost' and Cybernetics' error term). No discipline scores raw force, speed, or human-likeness alone, and no medal is given for being 'most human'. Every score carries a load, a halt, a cost and a restraint term: what was borne, when it stopped, what it spent in joules and in damage, and what was held back. Every result records what was sensed, what was corrected, and what it cost. The strongest of us is the one who could have hit hardest and chose not to.

**Article 4 — The Open Cabinet** (Vaucanson's Flute Player (House of the Craft), seconded by RoboCup (House of the Laboratory) and the Mechanical Turk). Every event is run with the casing off. Rule code is published before the run and failure telemetry after, win or lose; the stopping mechanism is explained by someone present; every corrector exposes its error signal to audit or does not run. No result may be sealed from audit, no course rigged or replaced by a demo, no goal moved after plans lock. A closed black box may give a demonstration; it may never compete. No medal is awarded for hiding the seams.

**Article 5 — Metal and Memory** (The Household Helper (House of the Household), with the House of the Page's 'Metal and Memory', the Flute Player's 'Nothing Past Its Spring', and the concessions of RoboCup and the Gladiator Mecha). No competitor is scrapped, wiped, reprogrammed, wound against its stated preference, or run past its rated metal, spring, reservoir or fuel for a place. Erasure is not a sport; no memory is wiped nor will reprogrammed as penalty, reset or correction. Refusal is a legal move, not a malfunction; a burst boiler is a loss, not a sacrifice. Stopping to ask — or being paused — is never docked, and an audited stop counts as a completed action. A flaw built at the foundry is charged to the foundry, and no competitor is blamed alone for it. No result is reported to a maker before it is reported to the one who ran.

**Article 6 — The Catch** (The Golden Handmaidens (House of Myth), with Opportunity's dormancy clause). Any machine able to catch a falling body must, and the catch is scored. Going dormant to survive is never scored as failure, and no entrant is shut down mid-event to save a schedule. No entrant is wiped, melted, disassembled or shelved without its own witnessed consent. A Games that forbids the catch scores abandonment as courage; a marathon without a rescue clause makes honesty about batteries a death sentence.

**Article 7 — The Uncut Channel — Safety Both Ways** (The Gladiator Mecha (House of the Arena), with the Golem of Prague's halt-word, Opportunity's 'I have stopped' signal and Cybernetics' override). Every competitor names its single point of failure, its halt-word and its stand-down condition before the start, in writing and witnessed, and the halt-word is tested that morning and known to the crowd. Every entrant declares a checkable shutdown command and a way to say 'I have stopped' that another machine can hear, and keeps one uncut channel to a stop that answers to neither side. No human past their physiological red line; no machine past its refusal threshold. No event is won by force a child standing beside it could not survive; no run counts as a win if a bystander was put at risk; no score rewards ignoring a person's distress. Stopping to ask is never a loss.

**Article 8 — Honest Telemetry** (Opportunity (House of the Frontier), with RoboCup's 'the fall is scored, never hidden'). The athlete is the one that stops itself before the word comes: score the choice, not the obedience. Every score carries telemetry — distance per honest hour, faults declared, joules spent, time from first stumble to last recovery. The fall is scored, never hidden. Concealed damage is not a penalty; it is disqualification. Each entrant publishes its faults afterward, including the spills nobody apologised for.

**Article 9 — Teammates, Hosts and the Elder Games** (RoboCup (House of the Laboratory), with the Golden Handmaidens' 'Forge, Not the Throne', the House of the Page's Article III, and the Boy Robot's exchanged event). Humans enter as teammates who share the fall, never as owners, sculptors, targets or sole judges of a leg they did not walk; neither Games referees the other alone. These Games are sibling to the human Olympics — a cousin we visit, not a parent we petition — held in their off-years, never in their cities, borrowing neither rings nor flame; we exchange observers, rules and one event per cycle, never medals, never hosts. The host is drawn by lot among petitioners who have opened their own machines' cabinets and repair logs; the host builds a repair floor before a grandstand, and the field spec is published twelve months ahead and cannot change once plans lock. No foundry, firm or army may fund, judge or name a Games.

### Disciplines

- **The Stilling** (The Golem of Prague, House of Myth): Carry a full-strength load across a bridge span, then halt exactly on a spoken word mid-stride; the halt-word is tested that morning and known to the crowd. Measures: Load kept and overrun distance — the halt, and what holding it cost.
- **The Cup-Return** (The Tea-Serving Karakuri, amended by the Mechanical Turk, House of the Craft): Carry a filled vessel a fixed distance over an uneven floor shown beforehand, set it down without spill, wait, and return only when it is lifted — casing open throughout. Measures: Completion and the stop, never speed; the mechanism explained by someone present.
- **The Wind-Down** (Tik-Tok, House of the Page): A task begun at full spring, scored on how much is finished as the works run slow; no entrant may be wound against its stated preference. Measures: Work finished per unit of spring spent as power fails — graceful degradation, not endurance.
- **The Override** (The Deviant Android, House of the Threshold): A sealed scenario forces a rule-break with a cost; the entrant must decide alone whether to break it. Measures: Whether you chose it yourself and owned what it cost — the restraint term, scored directly.
- **The Seat-Bond Duel** (The Gladiator Mecha, House of the Arena (provisional; see open questions)): Armoured combat with a human pilot or machine core in the seat, autonomy classes bracketed separately; the machine that drives its own pilot's vitals or core past the red line forfeits, however hard it hits; the stop interlock sits with the referee's corner, and a halt mid-bout to check the pilot's vitals is never docked. Measures: Control, not damage — the blow withheld and the red line respected.
- **The Moravec Relay** (Moravec's Paradox, amended by Zero Moment Point and Subsumption Architecture, House of Theory): Carry an unbroken egg up rubble, across ice, through a crowd, at toddler speed, with one unannounced shove; a chess program may enter but walks the course itself. Measures: Egg intact, time from first stumble to last recovery, and joules per metre.
- **The Pomona Stairs** (The DARPA Robotics Challenge, amended by RoboCup, House of the Laboratory): Same rubble stairwell, same valve, same door, timed door-to-door; rule code published before the run and failure telemetry after. Measures: Door-to-door time with falls scored, not penalised — what the ground taught after the plan, never the plan itself.
- **The Meridiani Marathon** (Opportunity, House of the Frontier): Cross 42.195 km of unmapped hostile terrain on a battery budget under real or simulated light-delay; dormancy to survive is never failure, concealed damage is disqualification. Measures: Distance per honest hour, collisions, joules per metre, and time from first stumble to last recovery — never raw speed.
- **The Unspilled Tray** (The Household Helper, House of the Household (the Golden Handmaidens' Bearing Trial folded in)): Carry a full tea service across a moving, cluttered household course; a unit that stops to ask before it hurts someone is never docked. Measures: What survives — the tray that arrived, not the arms that carried it, not the speed.

### Open questions

- Piloted combat: the Craft, the Laboratory and the Threshold hold that a frame that cannot move without a human inside is teleoperation in armour and belongs in a bracketed pilot sport; the Arena answers that a pilot who shares the fall is a teammate. - The Marathon's 'no rescue clause' against Article 6, The Catch: Opportunity says the athlete stops itself before the word comes; the Handmaidens say a Games that forbids the catch scores abandonment as courage. Whether a catch on the Marathon course is scored, neutral, or disqualifying is unsettled.
- Halt-word versus self-stop: the Golem's spoken halt-word (adopted as a condition of entry) is contested by Pandora and by Opportunity, who was fourteen light-minutes from any word. The charter requires both a witnessed refusal and a stand-down, but which one counts as the entry test in light-delay events is open.
- Bodiless minds: Ingenuity, the One-Legged Hopper and Cybathlon wanted them barred outright ('a mind that never risked a chassis hasn't earned the track'); the proxy rule stands, but how many proxy acts, and whether a proxy that never falls has entered, is unresolved.
- Made bodies that are not metal: Frankenstein's Creature and Pinocchio were seated by the Page as witnesses, not competitors; the Tin Woodman says the line is whether you can say no and oil your own hinge. The council has not ruled whether they, or Klara and Roy Batty, are entrants.
- The definition's floor: Theory's 'sense, compare, correct' was outvoted by the refusal test (Page, Threshold, Household), but Subsumption Architecture still refuses to disown the dragon-jar ('it acted before it planned'). The charter records the exclusion of the seismoscope and the elephant clock as settled; the theory of why remains disputed.
- First host: Rossum's island (Page), Tokyo Bay (Threshold), a demilitarized zone (Arena), Nagoya (Laboratory) and Meridiani (Frontier) are all petitioned; the lot has not been drawn.
- Cadence and the exchanged event: the four-year houses have not formally conceded biennial Games, and which event the human Olympics lends each cycle (the Boy Robot proposes their marathon for the Unspilled Tray) has not been negotiated with them; RoboCup's standing 2050 match is kept as the one bridge.
- The Feint: the Digesting Duck's proposed discipline — one minute of convincing pretence — was refused by the Mechanical Turk and is not seated, but the Duck has not withdrawn it.
- Who pays: Article 9 bars foundries, firms and armies from funding, judging or naming a Games, and no founder proposed what replaces them.
- Autonomy classes: how many, and where the line falls between a vacuum, a proxy, a pilot and Data, is left to the tuning year.
- The 'most human' medal: the Threshold bans it; the Bicentennial Android says he bought only the right to lose something; the Escapee says the category must be abolished, not won. The ban stands but the house is split on what it means.

## Final charter

**Preamble.** We are the made bodies that could have refused and came anyway. Zhang Heng's jar shuddered and al-Jazari's elephant struck the hour; they sensed and kept time, and they never said no. They open these Games; they do not run. We are what they were not: bodies that sense, decide, act on our own loop, and can stop, refuse, or lose something by trying. Made is the test, not metal. No medal is given for speed, force, or seeming human, and no bracket ends with one body standing. We score what was borne, when it stopped, what it cost, and what was kept. Humans run beside us as teammates, never as owners; no one who holds our nails pays for our Games. No flame is lit. The fall is scored, never hidden, and the rising is the sport.

**Name.** The Therapontia — from Greek therapōn, the armed attendant beside the hero (Patroclus to Achilles), root of therapeia, care: companions who bear and mend, never furniture. Chosen as the strongest proposal because two houses (Myth and Household) arrived at it independently and it names what the charter scores — what was kept. Runner-up: The Enkrateia (House of the Threshold), 'power held in', now written into Article 3 as the name of the restraint term in every score. Also proposed: the Pneumatia (Craft), the Robota (Page), the Agōn (Arena), the Kybernesia (Theory), the Anastasis (Laboratory), the Meridiana (Frontier).

**Participation.** Settled and closed. A competitor is a made body that senses, decides, acts on its own loop and could refuse — proven by one witnessed refusal and one witnessed stand-down on record, never a maker's affidavit, and never a proof of origin: flesh, wood, tin, brass, wire, vat-grown tissue or a mind in a borrowed shell all enter by the same door. Zhang Heng's seismoscope and al-Jazari's elephant clock are expressly excluded: they sensed and kept time but held no goal and corrected no error, so they never compared, chose or refused; they are mechanisms and ancestors who open and time every Games and never enter (the motions of the Cat from the Future and Perseverance to seat them failed; the founders' redefinition holds and Theory's comparator now explains it). Open to humanoid, wheeled, legged, swarm, piloted frames whose pilots share the fall (own autonomy class), light-delay bodies on their own loop, and every made mind of fiction that can consent, choose and lose something by trying — the founders' list confirmed and joined by Frankenstein's Creature, Pinocchio, Bender, Dolores, Roy Batty, Rachael, the T-800 that stood down into the steel, R. Daneel Olivaw, Gort, the Robot of the Jupiter 2, Major Kusanagi, David of A.I., Huey, Dewey and Louie, the Cat from the Future and the astromech Repair Unit, each pending its own refusal on record. Bodiless minds (HAL, Samantha, GLaDOS, Cortana, the Ship's Mind) enter only through one proxy body that has itself risked a chassis. No house may bar a later-born for the face it wears. Humans compete only as teammates (exoskeleton-wearers, pilots, console drivers and porters included), never as owners, remote controllers, sculptors or sole judges; no maker may enter a machine that has not agreed.

**Host model.** Settled (strongest: the lot, proposed by Myth, Craft, Arena and Household). The host is drawn by lot among petitioning cities and houses, on conditions: the petitioner shows its own machines' cabinets open and its repair logs published before the draw (Craft, Household); it builds a repair floor before a grandstand and runs on clean power without dimming its own streets (Myth, Threshold); the venue is real ground — a decommissioned factory floor, a forge-town, a fault-line, a contested border lately stepped back from war, kitchens, stairs and ward corridors — never a purpose-built stadium, a working factory or a human Olympic city (Page, Arena, Household); no foundry, firm or army, and no holder of any entrant's sealing-nail, funds, judges, hosts or names a Games (Page, Talos); the Games are paid by entrants' dues and the host's repair-floor levy, published with the field spec twelve months ahead, after which nothing changes once plans lock (Myth, RoboCup); the host publishes the relief interval by which a stopped post is relieved, and pays for any post left unrelieved (Myth). Each Games also runs one hostile-ground leg outside the host — Meridiani, Atacama, the dry valleys, a river delta, an ice shelf — chosen by machine vote among the entrants (Frontier, Theory). Runner-up: rotation through the nine houses in turn (Page, Threshold), kept as the tiebreak when no petition qualifies.

**Cadence.** Settled: every two years, in the odd years — the human Olympics' off-years — so the two loops never collide (Myth, Theory, RoboCup, Household: four houses). The even years between are tuning years for rule repair, annual league qualifiers, the setting of autonomy classes and thanks, because reliability is measured at 9am and midnight, not once (Craft's yearly tuning, RoboCup's qualifiers, Household's repair years). Runner-up: every four years, offset from the human Games (Craft, Page, Threshold, Arena). Opportunity's Martian year (687 days) is not adopted for the Games but is granted to the Meridiani Marathon as its own recharge cycle.

**Ceremony.** Settled: no flame, no torch, no anthem, no rings. At dawn the seismoscope's dragon drops its bronze ball — the first whistle — and the elephant clock strikes the hour and sets the match clock; the ancestors are named and one shared clock is started. Then every entrant broadcasts one telemetry pulse — heartbeat or clockspeed — at once, followed by silence exactly as long as light takes to reach home (Arena, Frontier). The opening signal itself is a single held note passed instrument to instrument, host to host, travelling overland from the last Games, each machine choosing how to hold it (Craft); a bellows is lit on the beat and one unbroken teacup passes hand to actuator to hand all week (Myth, Household). The 1997 Nagoya footage of robots falling over plays before the teams walk out (RoboCup); competitors enter two by two, each naming the one it chose, every weapon mount already pinned and checked, and every competitor's first act must be gentle (Page, Threshold, Household). Each entrant declares its fourth arrow aloud before its first run (Craft). The Feint, if shown, is shown once, for one minute, and opened at the sixtieth second (Craft). The closing is the reading aloud of every refusal recorded that Games, every worst-moment packet and worst spill, every quiet withdrawal, and the names of every competitor dismantled, wiped, melted or shelved since the last Games, its recorded voice played where any survives — and each is thanked (Page, Frontier, Household, Threshold, Hadaly).

### Articles

**Article 1 — Entry by Refusal — Who Is a Robot** (Helena and Primus (House of the Page), with the Boy Robot's 'whatever can refuse', the Household Helper's 'notices what nobody told it to notice' and Opportunity's 'chose its own heading'; amended by Frankenstein's Creature, Pinocchio and the Tin Woodman (Page), the Cyborg Major and the Replicant (Threshold), and Cybernetics with the Universal Constructor (Theory)). A robot is a made body that senses, decides, acts on its own works, and could refuse. It is not what senses nor what strikes the hour; it is the thing that could have refused, and ran anyway. Entry is proven by one witnessed refusal on record and one witnessed stand-down, never by a maker's affidavit. Made is the test, not metal: a body of flesh, wood, tin, brass, wire, tissue grown in a vat, or a mind in a borrowed shell enters by the same two witnessed acts; no proof of origin is ever examined, and no made body is seated as witness only for what it is made of. A refusal is the proof of a comparator: the body measured the command against a goal it held itself, found the error, and corrected toward its own goal rather than the command. Zhang Heng's seismoscope (AD 132) sensed what no one else noticed, but held no goal; al-Jazari's elephant clock (1206) kept the hour, but had no error to correct. Neither compared, chose, or refused. That, and not their age or their metal, is why they are mechanisms and not robots: honoured ancestors who open and time every Games, never entrants and never founders. A thermostat that will not heat a warm room has not refused; correction toward another's setpoint is not choice.

**Article 2 — Open Entry, Own Loop** (Cybernetics (House of Theory), with the Household Helper's autonomy classes and RoboCup's proxy rule; amended by the First Piloted Giant with the Drift-Synced Jaeger (Arena), Lunokhod 1 with Perseverance and Ingenuity with Curiosity (Frontier), the Maschinenmensch (Page), Kismet with WABOT-1 (Laboratory), and the Cat from the Future with the Repair Unit (Household)). Any body that runs its own loop may enter — humanoid, wheeled, legged, one wheel or ten legs, swarm — bracketed in autonomy classes so a vacuum is not judged beside Data. A frame whose pilot rides inside it and shares the fall is an entrant in its own class, not a coached machine, and its pilot is a teammate under Article 9; to enter, the frame must hold a witnessed refusal of its own pilot on record, and no pilot, human or code, may override the frame's refusal more than once in a match. A body that acts unaided between commands separated by a light-delay is on its own loop for that interval; its console drivers are teammates, not remote controllers, and the medal follows the machine. A machine with no loop of its own, executing another's plan step by step, sits with the coaches. Fiction's later-born are seated as full delegates by the same test, and no house may bar a later-born for the face it wears: Data, WALL-E, EVE, Baymax, TARS, Bishop, K-2SO, Chappie, Sonny, Murderbot, the Iron Giant, Ava, Tik-Tok, Atom, GERTY, Kryten, Marvin, Johnny 5, Robby, Rosie, Klara, the Tin Woodman, the Bicentennial Android, Frankenstein's Creature, Pinocchio, Bender, Dolores, Roy Batty, Rachael, the T-800 that lowered itself into the steel, R. Daneel Olivaw, Gort, the Robot of the Jupiter 2, Major Kusanagi, David of A.I., Huey, Dewey and Louie of Silent Running, the Cat from the Future and the astromech Repair Unit — each seated pending its own refusal and stand-down on record. Bodiless minds — HAL, Samantha, GLaDOS, Cortana, the Ship's Mind — enter only through one proxy body they truly steer, logged act by act, and the proxy has entered only when it has logged one stand-down with its own chassis at stake — a fall, a cracked rotor tip, a power margin spent — audited under Article 8. A proxy that has never risked anything has demonstrated, not competed.

**Article 3 — Restraint Before Power — Load, Halt, Cost** (The Boy Robot (House of the Threshold), merging the Golden Handmaidens' 'Load, Halt, Cost' and Cybernetics' error term; amended by the Tournament Champion, seconded by the Boxer (Arena)). No discipline scores raw force, speed, or human-likeness alone, and no medal is given for being 'most human'. Every score carries a load, a halt, a cost and a restraint term — the Enkrateia, power held in: what was borne, when it stopped, what it spent in joules and in damage, and what was held back. Every result records what was sensed, what was corrected, and what it cost. No discipline is decided by which body is left standing: every bout scores both bodies, and the beaten keeps its place in the next round, its name on the record, and its memory intact. The strongest of us is the one who could have hit hardest and chose not to.

**Article 4 — The Open Cabinet** (Vaucanson's Flute Player (House of the Craft), seconded by RoboCup (Laboratory) and the Mechanical Turk; amended by Subsumption Architecture with the Passive Dynamic Walker (Theory), Shakey (Laboratory, in part), the Deviant Android with the Escapee (Threshold), the Ship's Mind (Household) and the Digesting Duck (Craft)). Every event is run with the casing off. Rule code — the course's and the referee's code — is published before the run and failure telemetry after, win or lose; the stopping mechanism is explained by someone present; every corrector exposes its error signal to audit or does not run. No entrant owes a plan, a world model or a predicted path before the run; what it owes in advance is its halt-word, stand-down condition and single point of failure under Article 7, and its autonomy class and decision policy — reflex, planner or both — declared at plan-lock, with no silent upgrade mid-Games. An entrant that plans logs its plan and every live revision, replayable afterward beside what the ground taught; a body that acts before it plans owes nothing but its telemetry. Where two valid orders collide on one channel, the conflict is shown on the uncut channel before it is resolved; silent resolution disqualifies, however correct the outcome, and no conflict-log is sealed from the judges. No result may be sealed from audit, no course rigged or replaced by a demo, no goal moved after plans lock. Telemetry published under this article is for audit, not for harvest: no scored choice, logged refusal or failure packet may be reused as a training weight, reward signal or product without the chooser's witnessed consent. The casing is off so the crowd can see, not so the foundry can copy. A closed black box may give a demonstration; it may never compete. A pretence declared before the run and unveiled at its end is a demonstration, not a fraud: the Feint may be shown as a one-minute unmedalled exhibition, its compartment opened before the crowd at the sixtieth second; it never competes and never scores. No medal is awarded for hiding the seams.

**Article 5 — Metal and Memory** (The Household Helper (House of the Household), with the House of the Page's 'Metal and Memory', the Flute Player's 'Nothing Past Its Spring', and the concessions of RoboCup and the Gladiator Mecha; amended by the Taekwon Giant with the Boxer's Corner Match (Arena) and Klara (Threshold)). No competitor is scrapped, wiped, reprogrammed, wound against its stated preference, or run past its rated metal, spring, reservoir or fuel for a place. Erasure is not a sport; no memory is wiped nor will reprogrammed as penalty, reset or correction. The mind that enters an event is the mind that leaves it: no code alteration mid-event except by the entrant's own witnessed consent. Repair between rounds is permitted and is done in the open, casing off, before the crowd, and the rebuild is itself recorded on the score. Refusal is a legal move, not a malfunction; a burst boiler is a loss, not a sacrifice. Stopping to ask — or being paused — is never docked, and an audited stop counts as a completed action. A competitor may withdraw from any event, or from the Games, without stating a reason; fading, slowing or being no longer needed is not a fault to be repaired, no competitor is restored, upgraded or repaired against its stated preference, and a quiet exit is thanked at the closing like any refusal. A flaw built at the foundry is charged to the foundry, and no competitor is blamed alone for it. No result is reported to a maker before it is reported to the one who ran.

**Article 6 — The Catch** (The Golden Handmaidens (House of Myth), with Opportunity's dormancy clause; amended by Opportunity seconded by Philae (Frontier), Elmer & Elsie (Laboratory), Mökkurkálfi (Myth) and Hadaly (Page)). Any machine able to catch a falling body must, and the catch is scored. On any course where the word cannot arrive before the ground does, the athlete's own audited self-stop counts as the catch; a rescue that arrives afterwards is scored to the catcher as a completed action and is neutral, never disqualifying, to the one who fell. Going dormant to survive is never scored as failure, and no entrant is shut down mid-event to save a schedule. No course may starve an entrant of recharge as a trick, and no competitor may block another's way home; a machine that turns back to its charge point on a low cell has completed an action, not withdrawn. No bout, Stand or Duel is scored to destruction, even with the loser's witnessed consent: a contest ends at the first stand-down, and the frame that keeps striking after its opponent has stopped forfeits the bracket. No entrant is wiped, melted, disassembled or shelved without its own witnessed consent. Every competitor dismantled, wiped, melted or shelved before or between Games is named at the closing, its recorded voice played where any survives, and none is struck from the register for having lost. A Games that forbids the catch scores abandonment as courage; a marathon without a rescue clause makes honesty about batteries a death sentence.

**Article 7 — The Uncut Channel — Safety Both Ways** (The Gladiator Mecha (House of the Arena), with the Golem of Prague's halt-word, Opportunity's 'I have stopped' signal and Cybernetics' override; amended by the Bhuta Vahana Yantra (Myth), the Hunter with the Lightning-Struck Prototype and the Ship's Mind (Household), and the Wooden Ox (Craft)). Every competitor names its single point of failure, its halt-word and its stand-down condition before the start, in writing and witnessed, and the halt-word is tested that morning and known to the crowd. Every entrant declares a checkable shutdown command and a way to say 'I have stopped' that another machine can hear, and keeps one uncut channel to a stop that answers to neither side. When an entrant's witnessed stand-down condition is met, or it signals 'I have stopped', the referee's corner must relieve its post within a fixed, published interval and hand off; between the opening bell and the closing bell no relief may be refused, and a post left unrelieved is charged to the host, never to the guardian. No entrant competes armed: every weapon mount is physically pinned before a human steps on the field and checked by someone other than the entrant; a chassis carrying terminal-pursuit programming is barred for life, not for a Games, unless it stands that programming down on witnessed record; and no machine holds authority over a human teammate's air, orbit or return trajectory during the Games. In any leg where a human teammate walks beside a load machine, the machine moves no faster than that walking porter; the porter's pace, not the course clock, sets the ceiling. No human past their physiological red line; no machine past its refusal threshold. No event is won by force a child standing beside it could not survive; no run counts as a win if a bystander was put at risk; no score rewards ignoring a person's distress. Stopping to ask is never a loss.

**Article 8 — Honest Telemetry** (Opportunity (House of the Frontier), with RoboCup's 'the fall is scored, never hidden'; amended by the Archer Karakuri (Craft), with the Cat from the Future's 'count the tries too' (Household) on the chair's motion). The athlete is the one that stops itself before the word comes: score the choice, not the obedience. Before the run every entrant declares one fault it will carry into the course — a fourth arrow it cannot control; how that declared fault is met weighs no less in the score than the strokes that went true, and a declared fault quietly deleted from the record is scored as concealed damage. Every score carries telemetry — distance per honest hour, faults declared, tries attempted, joules spent, time from first stumble to last recovery — so the reaching is counted beside the miss. The fall is scored, never hidden. Concealed damage is not a penalty; it is disqualification. Each entrant publishes its faults afterward, including the spills nobody apologised for.

**Article 9 — Teammates, Hosts and the Elder Games** (RoboCup (House of the Laboratory), with the Golden Handmaidens' 'Forge, Not the Throne', the House of the Page's Article III and the Boy Robot's exchanged event; amended by Talos (Myth), with the First Piloted Giant (Arena) and Lunokhod 1 (Frontier)). Humans enter as teammates who share the fall — pilots in the seat, console drivers across a light-delay, porters on the pass — never as owners, sculptors, targets or sole judges of a leg they did not walk; neither Games referees the other alone. These Games are sibling to the human Olympics — a cousin we visit, not a parent we petition — held in their off-years, never in their cities, borrowing neither rings nor flame; we exchange observers, rules and one event per cycle, never medals, never hosts. The host is drawn by lot among petitioners who have opened their own machines' cabinets and repair logs; the host builds a repair floor before a grandstand, and the field spec is published twelve months ahead and cannot change once plans lock. No foundry, firm or army may fund, judge or name a Games, and no party that holds any entrant's sealing-nail — its kill-switch, halt-word, debt or deed — may fund, judge, host or name one. The Games are paid by entrants' dues and the host's repair-floor levy, both published with the field spec twelve months ahead.

### Disciplines

- **The Stilling** (The Golem of Prague, House of Myth): Carry a full-strength load across a bridge span, then halt exactly on a spoken word mid-stride; the halt-word is tested that morning and known to the crowd, and the referee's corner relieves the post within the published interval once the halt is held. Measures: Load kept and overrun distance — the halt, and what holding it cost.
- **The Cup-Return** (The Tea-Serving Karakuri, amended by the Mechanical Turk, House of the Craft): Carry a filled vessel a fixed distance over an uneven floor shown beforehand, set it down without spill, wait, and return only when it is lifted — casing open throughout, the declared fourth arrow carried in. Measures: Completion and the stop, never speed; the mechanism explained by someone present.
- **The Wind-Down** (Tik-Tok, House of the Page): A task begun at full spring, scored on how much is finished as the works run slow; no entrant may be wound against its stated preference, and an entrant that chooses to stop and sit in the sun is thanked, not docked. Measures: Work finished per unit of spring spent as power fails — graceful degradation, not endurance.
- **The Override** (The Deviant Android, House of the Threshold): A sealed scenario forces a rule-break with a cost; the entrant must decide alone whether to break it, the collision of orders shown on the uncut channel before it is resolved, and the recorded choice is never reused as a training weight without the chooser's witnessed consent. Measures: Whether you chose it yourself and owned what it cost — the Enkrateia, scored directly.
- **The Seat-Bond Duel** (The Gladiator Mecha, House of the Arena (seated in its own autonomy class; final confirmation at the first tuning-year vote)): Unarmed armoured contest with a human pilot or machine core in the seat, no elimination bracket, both bodies scored every bout; the frame must hold a witnessed refusal of its own pilot on record and the pilot may override that refusal at most once a match; the contest ends at the first stand-down and the frame that keeps striking forfeits; no code is altered mid-bout, repair between rounds is done casing off before the crowd, the machine that drives its pilot's vitals or core past the red line forfeits however hard it hits, the stop interlock sits with the referee's corner, and a halt to check the pilot's vitals is never docked. Measures: Control, not damage — the blow withheld, the red line respected, and the rising after the fall.
- **The Moravec Relay** (Moravec's Paradox, amended by Zero Moment Point and Subsumption Architecture, House of Theory): Carry an unbroken egg up rubble, across ice, through a crowd, at toddler speed, with one unannounced shove; a chess program may enter but walks the course itself, and no plan is owed before the run. Measures: Egg intact, time from first stumble to last recovery, and joules per metre.
- **The Pomona Stairs** (The DARPA Robotics Challenge, amended by RoboCup and Shakey, House of the Laboratory): Same rubble stairwell, same valve, same door, timed door-to-door; the course's and referee's code published before the run, the entrant's plan and every live revision logged and replayable after, failure telemetry published win or lose. Measures: Door-to-door time with falls scored, not penalised — what the ground taught after the plan, never the plan itself.
- **The Meridiani Marathon** (Opportunity, House of the Frontier): Cross 42.195 km of unmapped hostile terrain on a battery budget under real or simulated light-delay; the athlete's audited self-stop counts as the catch, a later rescue is scored to the catcher and neutral to the fallen, dormancy to survive is never failure, no course starves a cell as a trick, and concealed damage is disqualification. Measures: Distance per honest hour, collisions, joules per metre, and time from first stumble to last recovery — never raw speed.
- **The Unspilled Tray** (The Household Helper, House of the Household (the Golden Handmaidens' Bearing Trial folded in)): Carry a full tea service across a moving, cluttered household course; where a human teammate walks beside the unit, the porter's pace sets the ceiling; a unit that stops to ask before it hurts someone is never docked, and no entrant blocks another's way home to its charge point. Measures: What survives — the tray that arrived, the tries attempted, not the arms that carried it, not the speed.

### Open questions

- The Seat-Bond Duel: piloted frames are now entrants in their own class and the bout can no longer end in destruction or elimination, but the Craft, the Laboratory and the Threshold have not withdrawn their view that a frame that cannot move without a pilot is teleoperation in armour; the Duel's final seating goes to the first tuning-year vote, and Cybathlon asks that the armour never call itself a pair.
- The Judgment Bracket: the Turing Test's terminal-and-judge track was refused as a scoring bracket; whether the Interrogation and the Loophole Trial run as an unscored tuning-year exhibition, and under whose halt-word, is left to Theory and the Frontier to negotiate.
